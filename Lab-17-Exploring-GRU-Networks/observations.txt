GRU vs LSTM - Experimental Observations
=======================================

Dataset:
- 1,000 time-series records.
- Sine wave with random noise.
- Training data: first 80%.
- Testing data: last 20%.
- Sequence length: 50.
- Random seed: 42.

GRU Model:
- Two GRU layers.
- 50 units in each layer.
- Total parameters: 23,301.
- Training epochs: 10.
- Batch size: 64.
- Final training loss: approximately 0.0027.
- Final validation loss: approximately 0.0023.
- Test loss: 0.00226.
- Training time: 14.1 seconds.

LSTM Model:
- Two LSTM layers.
- 50 units in each layer.
- Total parameters: 30,651.
- Training epochs: 10.
- Batch size: 64.
- Final training loss: approximately 0.0021.
- Final validation loss: approximately 0.0019.
- Test loss: 0.00193.
- Training time: 12.5 seconds.

Observations:
1. Both models successfully learned the time-series pattern.
2. Both models showed decreasing training and validation losses.
3. LSTM achieved a lower test loss than GRU in this experiment.
4. GRU used fewer trainable parameters than LSTM.
5. LSTM trained faster in this particular run.
6. GRU can be a useful choice when a smaller model is preferred.
7. LSTM can be useful when its additional memory capability improves sequence learning.

Conclusion:
Both GRU and LSTM are effective recurrent neural network
architectures for sequence modeling. In this experiment,
LSTM achieved better test accuracy, while GRU used fewer
parameters. The best choice depends on the dataset,
accuracy requirements, model size, and computational cost.

Note:
Parameter counts and training times are based on the
installed Keras 3 implementation and this EC2 CPU environment.
